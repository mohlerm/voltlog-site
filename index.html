Preview · A logbook for electric Porsches

Every kilometre, every kilowatt-hour, every charge.

Voltlog keeps the full history of your Macan Electric, Taycan or Cayenne Electric and turns it into detailed statistics: where your consumption comes from, what each charge delivered, how tires and weather compare. It picks up where the My Porsche app stops. On the web, the iPhone and the iPad.

A personal project, shown here as a preview. It is not available to download. Feedback is welcome in the thread where you found this.

voltlog

Beyond the My Porsche app

What it adds

  • The full history

    Porsche keeps your trips for 12 months and the car's charges for about four weeks. Voltlog stores them for good, and a My Porsche export brings in older trips.

  • Much more detail

    Consumption by speed, trip length, temperature, weather, season and tire set, plus battery levels at each charge and the DC peak power by start level.

  • What a charge cost and lost

    Home charging from the wallbox, public charging from your invoices. Where both sides recorded a charge, Voltlog measures the loss between the grid and the battery.

Voltlog adds to the My Porsche app and does not replace it: it only reads data and never sends a command to the car.

Insights

What drives your consumption

Charts over the last twelve months or all data: when and how far you drive, consumption by speed, trip length, outside temperature, weather and season, energy and cost per month, battery levels when charging, and the peak power at each DC charge.

  • Records: the cheapest, longest, fastest and most efficient trips
  • Summer vs. winter tires, per set
  • Values above the columns, or hidden with one switch

Charging

All charging in one list

Home charging comes from the wallbox, public charging from the invoices and receipts of the charging provider, and the car adds what reached the battery. When two sources saw the same charge, Voltlog shows it once and measures the loss between the grid and the battery.

  • Each charge as a ring: what it added and how far that goes
  • Where the energy went, and the net charging time
  • Charging loss for AC and DC, measured on your own charges
  • Columns for the amount, the speed and what you paid
  • Import charging invoices and receipts as PDF
  • Estimates for charges the car did not report, clearly marked

Dashboard

The last 30 days at a glance

The dashboard opens with your last trip and last charge. Below them it shows your driving in columns, and on the same days the battery level: trips pull it down, charges lift it up, each with what it cost. Small icons switch the columns between consumption, distance, driving time and speed.

  • The last 30 days, this month or all time
  • Graphs and dials that build up, at the pace you set: on, instant or off
  • Electricity against petrol on one scale, with what you saved on energy
  • The range at your consumption and at today's temperature next to WLTP

Trips

Each trip with its cost and its weather

Every trip from the car, priced at what the energy in the battery cost when it was driven: the charges feed a running average, so a trip after a cheap home charge costs less than one after a fast charger on the motorway.

  • Consumption and speed as two dials, with the month's average
  • The share of a full battery it took, and what a petrol car would have cost
  • The weather where it started, from home or a saved location
  • Edit any value, add a note; edits survive the next sync
voltlog / trips

Reports

A month or a year at a glance

The monthly and the yearly report compare a period with the one before, from consumption to the savings against a petrol car, and share as an image that fits a phone screen.

  • Consumption per month, driving, costs and charging
  • Driving time, the share charged at home and the charging loss
  • A single trip or charge shares as an image the same way

Tires and locations

Which tires, and where trips started

The four pressures the car reports sit where the tires are, around the car seen from above, each with a marker against its ideal pressure. Log your swaps and see the mileage and consumption of each set, and each swap on its own with its dials. Name the places you drive from, and each trip gets the weather of where it started.

  • Summer and winter sets with their sizes and history
  • The swaps as a strip of the seasons
  • Distance and consumption per tire set and per location
voltlog / tires

Data checks

Gaps found, fixes suggested

Voltlog checks its own data. When the trips used more energy than the charges explain, it suggests the missing charge where the car stood longest. It also finds trips recorded twice and stays far from home. Nothing changes until you review a suggestion.

  • Missing charges from the energy balance between home charges
  • Stays away: set the start place for all their trips at once
  • A history of what was accepted and dismissed

Data sources

What it reads

Car: Porsche Connect
Required. Vehicle status, trips and the car's charging history. Built on a Macan 4S; the Taycan and the Cayenne Electric are not tested yet
Wallbox: Zaptec
Optional. Charging sessions of the home wallbox
Invoices and receipts
Optional. PDFs of Porsche Charging Service and Migros M-Charge, with what public charging cost
Trip export
Optional. CSV from My Porsche, for trips older than twelve months
Weather and exchange rates
Open-Meteo for the weather at your locations, the ECB rates via Frankfurter

Good to know

What to expect

Preview
Voltlog is not available to download, and whether it gets released is still open
Porsche access
Porsche has no public API. Voltlog uses the community library pyporscheconnectapi, so it can stop working whenever Porsche changes something
Costs
Costs mean electricity: what you paid to charge. Servicing, insurance, tires and depreciation are not included
Currencies
CHF, EUR, USD, GBP, SEK, NOK, DKK, PLN or CZK, with charges in another currency converted at the ECB rate of the day
Units and time
Metric or imperial (UK or US, with mi/kWh, mpg and psi), any time zone and a 24- or 12-hour clock, in the web and the iOS app

Privacy

Your data stays yours

Your own server
Voltlog is self-hosted for a single user, behind a login. There is no shared service in between
Your Porsche login
Stored encrypted on your server and used only to sign in to Porsche
No position tracking
Voltlog never asks the car for its location. Only the places you name go to the weather service
Manual sync
Nothing runs in the background; you decide when to sync
